  • October 31, 2026: PACT 2026 Concludes Four Days of Research, Collaboration, and Community in Chicago

    Thank you to everyone who joined PACT 2026 at the Discovery Partners Institute in Chicago on October 19–22! With 176 attendees across four days, the conference brought together researchers, students, and industry partners to share advances in parallel architectures and compilation techniques, exchange ideas, and build new collaborations. Photos from the event can be viewed [here] and access slides from the awards ceremony [here].

    The technical program featured research papers, posters, and the ACM Student Research Competition, alongside keynotes by Andrew A. Chien, Josep Torrellas, and Mary Hall, and an industry keynote by Samantika Sury. Workshops, tutorials, and the first National Science Data Fabric Summit opened the week, connecting the PACT community with emerging research directions and practical tools. The panel “Who Gets to Do Computing Research in 2036?” invited participants to consider the future of access and participation in computing research.

    The banquet at Willis Tower offered an opportunity to celebrate outstanding research and recognize the people who made the conference possible, followed by a visit to the Skydeck.

    Congratulations to our award recipients:

    • Best Paper Award: ESA: Improving GPU Utilization with Elastic Isolation for ML Inference Services, by Taeklim Kim, Saurabh Agarwal, Rachata Ausavarungnirun, Jayneel Gandhi, and Christopher J. Rossbach.
    • Best Artifact Award: SALT: Symbolic Analysis of Loop Tiling, by Yanghui Wu, Yifan Zhu, Yekai Pan, and Chen Ding.
    • ACM Student Research Competition — Undergraduate: First place, Nikhil Rout; second place, Jihyeon Hwang and Seungeun Lee; third place, Murat Toprak.
    • ACM Student Research Competition — Graduate: First place, Atharva Raut; second place, Befikir Bogale; third place, Eric Dubberstein.
    • Chameleon Travel Awardees: (i) Splyce: Kabilan Mahathevan, Poorna Gunathilaka, Kirshanthan Sundararajah (Virginia Tech) with the paper Splyce: SIMD Vectorization of Sparse Coiteration and (ii) Yanghui Wu, Yifan Zhu, Yekai Pan, Chen Ding (University of Rochester) with the paper SALT: Symbolic Analysis of Loop Tiling.

    We thank Google, HPE, the National Science Data Fabric (NSDF), and Qualcomm for their generous support, and recognize our sponsoring societies and supporting organizations: the Association for Computing Machinery (ACM), the ACM Special Interest Group on Computer Architecture (ACM SIGARCH), and the IEEE Computer Society. We also thank the Discovery Partners Institute and the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign for hosting PACT 2026.

    Support from Kate Keahey and the Chameleon Project, ACM SIGARCH, Google, HPE, and Qualcomm made 24 student travel awards possible, helping students participate in PACT 2026. We also thank Kate Keahey and the Chameleon Project for supporting reproducible research through artifact evaluation. NSDF supported nine early-career attendees at the NSDF Summit, held on Monday as part of PACT 2026’s workshops and tutorials day. Together, these contributions helped make the conference a welcoming and productive gathering.

    We close with special thanks to the people whose work made the conference possible, beginning with Technical Program Chair Jaejin Lee and Vice-Chair Bernhard Egger, together with the program committee and reviewers, for shaping the scientific program.

    Our gratitude also goes to Tanu Malik, Doru Adrian Thom Popovici, and Bogdan “Bo” Alexandru Stoica for artifact evaluation; Jay Lofstead for the ACM Student Research Competition; Jungwon Kim for publications; Sanjukta Bhowmick and Yuanchao Xu for workshops and tutorials; Antonino Tumeo for finance; and Zhao Yongwei and Jie Ren for publicity.

    We thank Kin Hong Ng for the conference website and, together with Kin Wai Ng, audiovisual support; Grace Wisser, Jessica Walker, and Brenda Peterson for local arrangements; and Steven, Befikir, and the Discovery Partners Institute volunteers and staff for their work throughout the event. We also recognize the workshop and tutorial organizers, session chairs, and the PACT Steering Committee, chaired by Lawrence Rauchwerger, for their service and guidance. General Chair Michela Taufer extends her appreciation to the entire organizing team, speakers, authors, and attendees.

    We also thank all workshop and tutorial organizers and presenters, paper and poster presenters, and undergraduate and graduate students who participated in the ACM Student Research Competition for the research, ideas, and discussions that made the program so engaging.

    Thank you for making PACT 2026 a memorable community gathering. From Chicago to Tokyo—we look forward to seeing you at PACT 2027!

  • August 5, 2026: PACT Registration Requirement

    Each accepted paper requires one full conference registration, regardless of who presents the paper. A full registration is required for each accepted paper. If an author has multiple accepted papers, a separate full registration is required for each paper. If the registered presenter is unable to attend (e.g., due to visa issues), the authors must arrange for a substitute presenter. Papers that do not meet the registration requirement or are not presented at the conference will be removed from the proceedings.

  • August 5, 2026: ACM Open Access Policy

    PACT is an ACM conference and follows ACM's Open Access publishing policy. Authors whose institutions participate in the ACM Open program are covered through their institutional agreement. Authors who are not covered may be required by ACM to pay an Open Access publication fee. This fee is determined by ACM, is separate from the conference registration fee, and is not managed by the conference organizers. Additional information is available on the ACM Open website.
